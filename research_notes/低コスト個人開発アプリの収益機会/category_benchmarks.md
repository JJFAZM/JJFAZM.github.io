# Category & Monetization Benchmarks for a Solo iOS App Targeting ~¥100,000/month (~$650-700 net)

Method note: WebFetch was egress-blocked for every primary source tried (revenuecat.com, adapty.io, saastr.com, singular.net, tasu.ai, ppc.land, pricepush newsletter). **Every figure below comes from search-engine snippets/summaries only and should be treated as UNVERIFIED until checked against the original report.** Figures repeated consistently across several independent snippets are flagged "(consistent across snippets)". Search date: 2026-09-23.

Target arithmetic: ¥100,000 ≈ $650-700 net. Under Apple's Small Business Program (15% commission, standard knowledge, not re-verified here), that is roughly $770-820/month gross proceeds before tax.

---

## Q1. Probability of reaching ~$1K MRR, and which categories get there (RevenueCat 2024-2026)

### Takeaway
Only ~17% of subscription apps reach $1K MRR within two years, and ~4.6-5% reach $10K. $700/month net sits just below the $1K MRR line, so the base rate for a typical subscription app hitting the goal is roughly 1 in 5 to 1 in 6. Health & Fitness has the best per-install monetization, Utilities has the best subscriber retention, and Photo & Video, Gaming and Entertainment trail on conversion.

### Cited Findings
- 17.3% of subscription apps reach $1K MRR within two years of launch. Only 4.6% reach $10K MRR, and the ~75% drop-off between $1K and $10K is where most apps stall (RevenueCat SOSA 2026, UNVERIFIED snippet, consistent across snippets) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top)
- The 2024 report (~30,000 apps) found that only 17.2% of apps exceeded $1,000 in monthly revenue — [GIGAZINE on RevenueCat 2024](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240313-apps-subscription-stats/)
- The 2025 report says "nearly 20% reach $1,000 in revenue while 5% reach $10,000." For apps that do reach $1K MRR, the median time is 60 days across categories: Gaming takes 32 days and Business 113 days (UNVERIFIED snippet) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- An aggregator states that 57.7% of new subscription apps never cross $1,000 in *total* revenue, and 22.4% earn between $1K and $10K (UNVERIFIED; attributed to RevenueCat and Adapty) — [Adapty blog, via search summary](https://adapty.io/blog/how-much-does-a-subscription-app-make/)
- Aggregator claim: "true median monthly revenue dropped 22% YoY from $627 to $492" while 31% more new apps launched in 2025 than in 2024 (UNVERIFIED, original source unclear) — [forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/app-revenue-potential)
- Tasu.ai headline: "the median [subscription app] is $72 a month" (UNVERIFIED; headline only) — [tasu.ai](https://tasu.ai/library/how-much-money-do-subscription-apps-make)
- Revenue concentration: the top 10% of apps take 94.5-95% of subscription revenue, up from 92.7% in 2023 (Adapty 2026, 16,000 apps, $3B revenue) — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/app-subscription-revenue-concentration/); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/95-of-app-subscription-revenue-goes-to-top-10-adaptys-2026-benchmark-report/)
- Health & Fitness, Photo & Video and Utilities show "some of the most consistent growth to later milestones" beyond $1K. Health & Fitness and Utilities still take more than 100 days to reach $10K. Business is described as an overlooked category that "monetizes and retains users the most" (UNVERIFIED snippets) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- The 2026 dataset covers more than 115,000 apps, more than $16B in revenue and more than 1B transactions. Most period metrics are for calendar 2025 — [RevenueCat 2026 blog summary](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)

### Inferences
- The base-rate probability that a new subscription app reaches ~$700 net/month within 2 years is roughly 18-22%. The $1K MRR rate is ~17%, and $820 gross is slightly easier to reach. The distribution is heavily skewed, so the median app earns far less (estimates run from $72 to $492/month depending on the source and definition).
- These base rates include funded teams and apps that use paid UA. A zero-ad-spend solo app likely sits below the base rate unless it has a strong search niche.

### Gaps
- The share of apps reaching $1K MRR **by category** could not be retrieved; the category pages were blocked.
- There is no RevenueCat split of the milestone rate for solo or indie developers versus teams.

## Q2. Revenue per install (RPI) and conversion by category, paywall type and plan length

### Takeaway
Health & Fitness is the strongest category on RPI and trial conversion, followed by Education and Business. Photo & Video and Gaming are the weakest converters. A hard paywall yields roughly 5x the download-to-paid conversion of freemium. Annual plans dominate Health & Fitness, while weekly plans dominate the overall revenue mix (driven by Gaming, AI and Photo apps).

### Cited Findings
- **Median RPI at day 60 (2026 report):** all categories about $0.34, Health & Fitness about $0.66, Gaming about $0.14. Education is about $0.30 at day 14 (UNVERIFIED snippet) — [RevenueCat 2026 blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [tasu.ai RPI by category](https://tasu.ai/library/app-category-revenue-per-install-benchmark)
- Tasu.ai headline: "Revenue per install by category: Health & Fitness leads at $0.48, Gaming at $0.08." This is probably an earlier-day metric and conflicts with the $0.66 day-60 figure above (UNVERIFIED) — [tasu.ai](https://tasu.ai/library/app-category-revenue-per-install-benchmark)
- **2025 report:** overall D60 median RPI is $0.31, and AI apps average $0.63 or more. Health & Fitness has the highest P90 RPI at $4.19. Education's P90 is $3.13, about 8x its median of $0.40 (UNVERIFIED) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- **Paywall type:** hard-paywall apps reach a median 10.7% download-to-paid rate by day 35, versus 2.1% for freemium (about 5x) (consistent across snippets) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [neoads substack](https://neoads.substack.com/p/hard-paywalls-convert-less-but-earn); [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/hard-paywall-vs-freemium-2026). One secondary snippet mislabels the 2.1% as "free trials", so treat it as freemium per the majority of sources. Hard paywalls are also said to deliver about 8x higher RPI at day 60 ($3.09 cited; UNVERIFIED and likely an upper-quartile or mean figure).
- **Trial-to-paid by category (2026):** median 37.7% for Health & Fitness (top quartile above 51.4%) and 22.2% for Photo & Video, the lowest (top quartile above 33.1%). Travel and Health & Fitness lead; Photo & Video and Gaming lag (UNVERIFIED) — [tasu.ai trial benchmark](https://tasu.ai/library/trial-to-paid-conversion-rate-benchmark); [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- **Adapty 2026:** global average 10.9% install-to-trial and 25.6% trial-to-paid. Health & Fitness leads trial-to-paid at 35.0% and Entertainment trails at 19.1%. First-renewal retention is highest in Utilities (58.1%) and lowest in Health & Fitness (30.3%) — [Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)
- **Plan mix by category:** Gaming about 82% weekly, Productivity about 77% monthly, Health & Fitness about 68% annual. Health & Fitness month-1 realized LTV is $24.23 versus $8.41 for Gaming (RevenueCat 2026, UNVERIFIED) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 via search](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps). Adapty's figure differs: Health & Fitness is the only category where annual plans dominate, at 60.6% — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- **Plan revenue share (Adapty, 2025):** weekly plans rose to 55.6% of revenue. Median prices are $7.48/week, $12.99/month and $38.42/year — [Adapty 2026 findings](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- Cheap annual plans retain up to 36%, while expensive monthly plans retain only about 6.7% (Japanese summary of Adapty/RevenueCat, UNVERIFIED) — [Adapty JP](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)
- Longer trials (14-30 days) convert better, even though apps are shortening their trials (RevenueCat 2026, UNVERIFIED) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)

### Inferences
- **Install volume needed for ~$820 gross/month (steady state):** with a cumulative lifetime RPI of about $0.5-0.7 (a rough extrapolation of the D60 median of $0.34, not sourced), you need about 1,200-1,600 organic installs/month at the median. A Health & Fitness app at median (D60 about $0.66, higher LTV) would need about 800-1,000 per month. A Gaming-like or freemium app with a weak paywall would need 3,000 or more. Treat these as order-of-magnitude figures only.
- **Category ranking by monetization efficiency per install** (subscription model): Health & Fitness > Business ≈ Education (high variance) > Productivity/Utilities (Utilities has the best retention) > Lifestyle > Photo & Video > Entertainment/Gaming. For a zero-server solo app, Utilities and Productivity offer good retention and search-driven demand, while Health & Fitness offers the highest conversion but is crowded.
- A hard paywall, or at least an aggressive paywall with a trial, is the single strongest lever available to a small-volume app.

### Gaps
- There are no retrieved D60 RPI medians for Utilities, Productivity, Photo & Video or Lifestyle individually.
- There are no hard-versus-soft paywall results split by category.
- The price-point conversion curve (conversion at different price levels) was not retrieved.

## Q3. One-time / lifetime / paid upfront versus subscription versus ads

### Takeaway
Subscriptions still dominate, but one-time purchases (including lifetime unlocks) are a growing ~10% of revenue on Adapty's platform. The 2025-2026 data offers no rigorous head-to-head of paid-upfront versus subscription for indie apps. Ad revenue at small scale is low because banner eCPMs are about $0.5-1.5.

### Cited Findings
- One-time purchases (consumables and lifetime) grew from 6.4% of revenue in 2023 to 10.3% in 2025 on the Adapty platform — [Adapty 2026 findings](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/95-of-app-subscription-revenue-goes-to-top-10-adaptys-2026-benchmark-report/)
- RevenueCat's 2025 report is described as noting a "shift away from pure subscriptions", meaning hybrid models (subscription plus lifetime or consumables) — [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions)
- An indie guide claims "subscription apps generate 4.5x more lifetime revenue per user than one-time purchase apps" and that "the median indie iOS app earns <$500/mo", both attributed to RevenueCat. **This is low-credibility and UNVERIFIED:** I could not trace either claim to a RevenueCat figure, and RevenueCat's data covers subscription apps only — [theswiftk.it](https://theswiftk.it.com/blog/how-to-monetize-ios-app-indie-developer)
- Common indie practitioner advice: annual plan of $20-40 as the core, a lifetime option at 3-4x the annual price as an anchor, and paid upfront for single-purpose utilities (opinion, not data) — [appopportunity](https://appopportunity.com/blog/indie-app-revenue-models-2026); [theswiftk.it](https://theswiftk.it.com/blog/how-to-monetize-ios-app-indie-developer)
- **Ad eCPM benchmarks:** iOS banner about $0.45 in the US. The banner range is $0.50-1.50 in tier-1 markets including Japan. US iOS rewarded video about $19.63. On iOS in Q4 2024, the top interstitial markets were the US ($14.32), UK ($10.38) and South Korea ($10.04) — [revenuelab.fyi](https://www.revenuelab.fyi/blog/admob-ecpm-benchmarks-2026); [Playwire](https://www.playwire.com/blog/admob-ecpm-benchmarks-what-publishers-should-expect); [MonetizeMore](https://www.monetizemore.com/blog/ecpm-insights/)
- Japan is described as "the powerhouse in interstitial ads", peaking at $10.78 in February 2025 against a US peak of $8.57, but it fell about 34.7% in Q4 2025 (Bidlogic, network-specific, UNVERIFIED) — [Bidlogic Q4 2025](https://bidlogic.io/2026/01/30/what-happened-to-mobile-app-ecpms-in-q4-2025/); [Bidlogic H1 2025](https://bidlogic.io/2025/07/25/ecpm-growth-in-mobile-apps-q1-q2-2025-analysis-and-insights/)

### Inferences
- **Ads-only arithmetic:** $820/month from banners at about $1 eCPM needs about 820,000 impressions/month, or about 27,000 per day, which is roughly 2,000-3,000 DAU at 10-15 impressions each. Using interstitials or rewarded ads at $8-15 eCPM cuts that to a few hundred DAU but hurts UX and retention. For a non-game utility, ads-only is feasible only with high-frequency daily use (timers, calculators, trackers).
- Lifetime or paid-upfront is lower-risk for users and fits "zero running cost" apps (no server means no ongoing cost to justify a subscription). The trade-off is that revenue then depends entirely on a steady flow of new installs.

### Gaps
- There is no 2025-2026 dataset comparing the revenue distribution of paid-upfront iOS apps against subscription apps. Appfigures pages with that kind of breakdown were not accessible.
- There is no AdMob-official Japan-versus-US eCPM for non-game utility apps.

## Q4. Organic (ASO) discovery share and search-driven versus viral categories

### Takeaway
Search is the dominant organic channel on iOS. Apple has said about 65% of downloads follow a search. Search-intent utility categories (Utilities, Productivity, Health & Fitness trackers, Education and reference) are the best fit for zero-ad-spend apps. Social, Photo & Video and entertainment apps depend more on virality and paid acquisition.

### Cited Findings
- About 65% of App Store downloads happen directly after a search, and about 70% of App Store visitors use search to find apps (Apple figures as repeated by ASO vendors; the original Apple statement is older) — [AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/what-is-app-store-optimization-and-why-is-aso-important); [MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/app-store-ranking-factors/)
- 2026 ranking behavior: Custom Product Pages now appear in organic search, and retention influences rankings (vendor opinion) — [ASOMobile](https://asomobile.net/en/blog/app-store-listing-2026-what-actually-affects-rankings/)
- The App Store logged its biggest release year in nearly a decade in 2025, meaning more competition for search slots — [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20251205?f=2)

### Inferences
- For a no-ad-spend app, the realistic channel is long-tail keyword search. Categories where users search for a specific function (e.g., "habit tracker", "PDF scanner", "interval timer", "kanji flashcards") offer the most predictable organic flow.
- Photo & Video and Lifestyle "trend" apps have weak trial conversion and rely on social virality, which is unpredictable for a solo developer.

### Gaps
- No category-level split of search versus browse versus referral downloads was found. Apple does not publish one, and Appfigures/AppTweak category analyses were not retrieved.

## Q5. Japan-specific subscription behavior and monetization

### Takeaway
Japan is one of the best markets for subscription retention. It has the highest year-1 renewal rates worldwide, the highest median annual price, and is among the fastest-growing App Store markets. Its interstitial eCPMs have historically matched or beaten the US, though they are volatile.

### Cited Findings
- Year-1 retention in Japan is the world's highest: 42% for annual, 26% for monthly and 10% for weekly subscriptions (RevenueCat, UNVERIFIED snippet) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [GIGAZINE on 2024 report](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240313-apps-subscription-stats/)
- Japan outperforms EMEA in revenue per download on Google Play (RevenueCat, UNVERIFIED) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- **Regional RPI:** North America median RPI is $0.39 overall and $0.55 at D60, versus $0.11 for India and Southeast Asia (a 5x gap). North America's D35 download-to-paid median is 2.6%, versus 1.4% for India and Southeast Asia. North America and Asia-Pacific lead on RPI — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- Japan has the highest median annual plan price at $54.59, and is one of the three fastest-growing App Store subscription markets along with Mexico and Turkey (Adapty 2026) — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/fastest-growing-app-markets-2026/); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/95-of-app-subscription-revenue-goes-to-top-10-adaptys-2026-benchmark-report/)
- Japan generated about $11B in IAP revenue in 2025, second in Asia only to China iOS. Globally, non-game apps overtook games in IAP revenue in 2025 (Sensor Tower) — [Sensor Tower Q1 2025 DMI](https://sensortower.com/blog/q1-2025-digital-market-index); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/global-mobile-in-app-purchase-revenue-hit-167bn-in-2025/)

### Inferences
- A Japanese-language app sold in the Japanese App Store benefits from high willingness to pay for annual plans and low churn. That is favorable for accumulating MRR slowly without paid UA, since fewer new subscribers are needed to replace churn.
- A modest ¥3,000-6,000/year annual plan with Japan's retention profile would need roughly 200-400 active annual subscribers to reach ¥100k/month net. This is an estimate, not a sourced figure.

### Gaps
- No Japan-specific download-to-paid or RPI numbers were found, only retention and pricing.
- No Japan-specific breakdown of which categories monetize best was found.
- No AdMob-official Japan iOS non-game eCPM was found.

# Competitors for a "favorite face analysis" tool (「あなたは〇〇フェチ」 from multiple faces the user likes)

Research date: 2026-09-23. Method: web search snippets only. App Store, Google Play, findrjp.com, beautyscope.jp, chizaizukan.com and realworldappeal.com were all blocked by the egress proxy, so ratings, review counts, and last-update dates could NOT be read directly. Figures marked UNVERIFIED come from search snippets or self-reported/secondary sources.

## Q1. Who directly analyzes "your type" from faces you like (preferred faces, not your own)?

### Takeaway
No product found that does exactly this: let a user upload several photos of real faces they like, pull out the common features (part shapes, proportions, how parts relate), and name a 「〇〇フェチ」 type. The nearest products are (a) Japanese quiz sites where you pick from preset faces (Findr, 診断メーカー, 暇つぶし診断), (b) FaiCE (JP), which learns a preference from 30 AI-generated faces and outputs a generated "ideal face" rather than an explanation in words, and (c) dating apps like Iris, which learn attraction from LIKE/PASS ratings but keep the model hidden and use it only for matching.

### Inferences
- The direct-competitor slot looks empty in both JP and EN. The core job is "upload real faces you like → explain the common features → name a fetish type". Existing tools either (a) restrict you to preset faces, (b) output a generated face instead of words, or (c) hide the preference model inside matching.
- The main risk runs the other way. Dating apps (Tinder Chemistry, Iris) already have preference data at scale and could add a "your type" readout cheaply. Iris's small scale suggests preference-trained matching alone has not been a big hit.
- FaiCE is the closest JP technical analogue. The lack of any traffic or virality evidence for it is a warning sign: image-generation-based preference discovery did not take off (UNVERIFIED; absence of evidence).

## Q2. Adjacent categories: self-face analysis (顔タイプ診断, looksmaxxing, look-alike) and their traction

### Takeaway
Self-face analysis is where the money and virality are. Umax reportedly makes ~$500K/month on a $3.99/week subscription, and Japanese look-alike and face-score apps have topped App Store charts. The whole category, though, is built on "rate/diagnose MY face", and it draws mental-health criticism.

### Inferences
- Adjacent tools get their virality from two things: a shareable label (顔タイプ名, look-alike celebrity, score) and a single-selfie input. The target product needs several photos, which adds friction. A name like 「〇〇フェチ」 could supply the shareable label that JP diagnosis culture rewards.
- Monetization precedent: the Umax-style free-scan / paid-result weekly subscription works for self-improvement anxiety. It is unclear whether curiosity about "my type" would support it. There is no evidence anyone pays for preference analysis on its own (negative evidence: every preference quiz found is free and ad-supported).
- Differentiation for the target: the preference side (others' faces), explanation at the level of parts and proportions, and JP "フェチ" naming. None of the competitors found combine all three.

### Gaps
- No Sensor Tower or Appfigures JP download/revenue numbers for フェイスタグ, FaceScore or そっくりさん (pages blocked or not surfaced).
- No store data for Glowy or Looksmax AI (JP), and no current 2026 Umax numbers. The latest hard-ish figures are the mid-2024 self-reported ones.
- Legal/privacy stance on uploading third-party faces (celebrities or acquaintances) was not researched here. It is likely a key risk: using celebrity images may raise portrait-rights (肖像権) and publicity-rights (パブリシティ権) issues in JP.
- TikTok hashtag view counts for #好きな顔 / #好みの顔 / #フェチ診断 were not retrievable.
